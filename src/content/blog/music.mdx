date: 2026-05-07
title: Music
description: Where words leave off, music begins.
---

Fun isn't the opposite of serious. It's the deepest form of it.

I was fifteen when I first understood that there existed a place you could go without moving. Not a physical place, not even quite a mental one. Something closer to a state of being, available only through sound, through the act of making it, through the particular and private miracle of turning nothing into something that had never existed before.

I didn't know then what I was learning. I only knew that when I was making music, I wasn't anywhere else. Not in my past, not in my future, not in the ordinary gravity of being a person in the world with all that entails. I was only there, in that moment, following something I couldn't fully see but could somehow hear ahead of me.

That's a gift of almost unreasonable proportions. And most people who make things, truly make things, know exactly what I'm describing. That state where the work stops feeling like work. Where hours pass without your permission. Where you're not deciding what comes next so much as discovering it, as though it were already there waiting for you to find it.

I've come to believe that this isn't a side effect of good work. It's the condition for it. The best things I've ever made didn't come from effort alone. They came from that particular quality of attention that's only possible when you're genuinely, almost helplessly, interested in what you're doing.

So whatever you make, however long you've been making it, I want to ask you one thing. Not whether it's good, not whether it's going anywhere, not whether it's worth your time. Just this: does it still feel like that? Like something you're following rather than forcing. Like a place you go to rather than a task you complete.

If it does, protect that feeling above everything else. It's more valuable than any outcome it might produce.

And if it doesn't, it may be worth asking when it stopped, and what it would take to find your way back.

Because the work that lasts, the work that means something, almost always begins in the same place. Not in ambition. Not in strategy. Not in the desire to be seen.

In someone, somewhere, making something for the purest reason there is.

Because they couldn't imagine not making it.
