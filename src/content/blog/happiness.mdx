title: The quiet kind of happy
description: Happiness lives in the details
---

I have come to believe, though perhaps later than I should have, that us, humans aren't very good at recognizing the good things while they're still with us.

There is a kind of restlessness in the way most of us move through our days. Always oriented toward something just ahead, a goal, an arrival, a version of life that will finally feel settled. And so we are often somewhere else entirely when the better moments find us. Not absent through any fault of character, just elsewhere. Thinking about tomorrow, or replaying yesterday, while today quietly passes.

I think about an ordinary evening. Nothing remarkable about it on the surface. A meal you actually enjoyed. A conversation that went longer than planned because neither of you wanted it to end. The light outside doing something you almost stopped to look at. A song that found you at exactly the right moment. None of these things announce themselves. They don't ask to be noticed. And so, most of the time, we don't notice them.

And yet those are precisely the moments we return to, years later, with a kind of warmth we didn't think to feel at the time.

That is the thing about small pleasures. They are not small at all. We just treat them that way, rationing our attention for the occasions we've decided in advance are worth it. The milestones, the achievements, the moments that look good in retrospect. But happiness doesn't seem to work like that, or at least it hasn't in my experience. It doesn't wait for the right occasion. It shows up on a Tuesday, in the middle of something ordinary, and it leaves quietly if you're not paying attention.

The past pulls at us with regret. The future pulls at us with anxiety. And the present, which is the only place anything is actually happening, gets what's left over. Which is often not very much.

I don't think the answer is complicated. It rarely is. It's not about having more, or getting somewhere better, or waiting until things are easier. It's about deciding that what's already here is worth your full attention. The people around you. The food in front of you. The conversation you're half in. The small, unremarkable, irreplaceable texture of an ordinary day.

Because it's not how much we have that makes us happy. It's how much we actually let ourselves enjoy it.

And there is always more to enjoy than we think, if we would only stop long enough to look.
