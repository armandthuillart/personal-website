I started [Dimsight](https://dimsight.com) with zero knowledge of building a company. I knew how to make software. I didn't know how to find customers, run a sales call, set a price, or follow up without feeling awkward.

At first I thought a good demo would sell it. I opened the 3D model and explained what it could do. The buyer was thinking about the machine, the price, and whether it would work in their factory. A €200,000 custom machine is hard to approve from a PDF and a few screenshots. If the client asks for another angle, sales often has to go back to engineering. The client waits while everyone tries to make the proposal easier to understand.

That became the way I explain Dimsight. A manufacturer can send the client one link to inspect the machine in a browser. The point is to help the sales conversation: the client can see what is being proposed, ask questions, and make a decision with less guesswork.

I had to learn how to sell. I began asking manufacturers how they showed custom products, what clients needed to see before they would sign, and how the conversation changed when the client could not picture the machine. I used to talk too much about the product. The good conversations started when I listened longer.

A company needs more than an app. I am learning to find the right customers, explain the problem clearly, decide what to charge, follow up, and hear no without taking it personally. I started with no idea how any of that worked. I'm building Dimsight one conversation at a time.
